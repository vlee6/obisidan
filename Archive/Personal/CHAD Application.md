I attended an elementary school in Boston's Chinatown. The afterschool and summer programs my parents enrolled me in shaped who I am. I volunteered at PBHA's CHAP for nearly three years, and I hope to see some familiar faces in CHAD.

**Tell us about the community that has impacted you most. How has this community shaped you, and how have you contributed to shaping the community? How will your experiences in this community inform your work as an SC? (200 words or less)**

Afterschool programs are a big part of who I am today.

When asked what got me into programming, my mind jumps to countless hours spent in afterschool dragging around colored pseudo-code blocks in Scratch. Now, I'm concentrating in computer science. I credit my love of making things to the counselor who taught me how to fold a cat out of a square sheet of paper. The peers I met in afterschool remain some of my best friends.

I pay it forward as a junior counselor for PHBA's Chinatown Afterschool Program (CHAP). With my own interest in STEM budding from afterschool, my goal is to spark the same interest through hands-on robotics kits. The universal language of soccer is my way of building trust and teamwork with a pair of ESL students. A whimsical magic trick—guessing a chosen card by its marked back—can spark laughter and curiosity, drawing shy students out of their shells.

CHAP has taught me to be a creative communicator using teaching moments, shared interests, and simple fun to bridge gaps. I hope to bring my CHAP energy to CHAD and continue embodying role models who have helped me become who I am today.

**How do you imagine building community among children in your camp group and what outcomes (academic, interpersonal, etc.) will you focus on? (150 words or less)**

The draw of a challenge brings students together in a way unlike any other. Whether it be in a fearsome soccer match against all the Senior Counselors or assembling a 3D printed scissor lift mechanism, students find a unique sense of camaraderie given a task that is insurmountable alone.

But there will always be students who initially feel hesitant to join. Taking small steps like learning their names and interests or engaging in small conversation and games goes a long way in making them feel welcomed.

Inspired by my own time in afterschool and summer programs, I hope to inspire kids to love learning even outside the classroom. Fighting summer learning loss doesn't have to be synonymous with tedius worksheets: making slime, playing word games, and even building a Domino laying robot can bring unique opportunities for students to grow and discover their interests.
