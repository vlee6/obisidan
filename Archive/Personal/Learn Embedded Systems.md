Petition for 1410:

I'm a rising Sophomore planning on concentrating in computer science, with particular interests in systems programming and hobby electronics. I have taken CS61, CS1200, and program and design PCBs in my free time, mostly working with ESP32s and more recently the Nordic nRF52840.

My brother, who studies ECE at Carnegie Mellon, first introduced me to FPGAs through an ECE Capstone called RetroCore, where a team of students emulated the hardware of a Game Boy on an FPGA. While RetroCore was interesting, I later came across a project that used an FPGA to control an array of transducers to levitate a small particle to create a volumetric display (VDATP), as well as an acquaintance's custom medical device that uses an FPGA to quickly process large volumes of data. Seeing how FPGAs dramatically outperform microcontollers when it comes to high throughput data processing, I'm determined to add FPGA programming to my skill set.

Learning a hardware description language like Verilog feels like a natural next step for me to not only expand what I can build, but also deepen my understanding of the layer below what I currently think of as "bare metal" programming in C++.

**What questions did you ask and what did you learn about the concentration/secondary field?**

I was unsure whether to concentrate in computer science or electrical engineering since both fields interest me, but Bobby helped me decide to concentrate in computer science having studied it in his undergrad. He explained that the ABET accredited track for electrical engineering wouldn't leave many free courses to pursue computer science, so it would unwise especially if I (1) wanted to study systems programming (2) explore other electives like biomedical engineering or semiconductor fabrication. 

He explained that, since I wanted to go into embedded systems programming or software engineering, electrical engineering is something I can pursue in extracurriculars, personal projects, or elective courses rather than my concentration.
