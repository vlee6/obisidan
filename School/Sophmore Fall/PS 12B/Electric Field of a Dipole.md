### Dipole moment:
$$\vec{p} = q\vec{s}$$
- Let $s$ be the vector pointing from the negative charge to the positive charge. The magnitude of $s$ is the distance between the negative charge to the positive charge.

### Along the axis of the dipole

![[Drawing 2026-09-19 16.19.45.excalidraw|800]]

Instead of calculating two seperate electric fields, if $x \gg s$, the electric field of a dipole is:

$$
\frac{2kqs}{x^3} \hat{p}
$$
- Let $q$ be the charges of the dipole
- Let $s$ is the distance between the two dipoles
- Let $x$ is the distance of the test point from the dipole
- Let $\hat{p}$ be the unit vector in the direction of the dipole moment

The electric field of a dipole along its axis points in the direction of the dipole moment.

### Perpindicular to the axis of the dipole
If $y \gg s$, the electric field of a dipole is:

![[Drawing 2026-09-19 17.13.34.excalidraw|300]]

$$
-\frac{kqs}{y^3}\hat{p}
$$

- Let $q$ be the charges of the dipole
- Let $s$ is the distance between the two dipoles
- Let $x$ is the distance of the test point from the dipole
- Let $\hat{p}$ be the unit vector in the direction of the dipole moment

The electric field of a dipole along its perpindicular bisector points in the direction of the dipole moment.
