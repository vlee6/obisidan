The electric flux of an enclosed surface that encloses no charge is 0.

The electric flux of a surface $A$ that encloses a charge such that the field everywhere on the surface of $A$ is constant and the field is perpendicular to the surface is just:

$$
\Phi = EA
$$

Gauss' Law, applicable when a closed surface encloses a charge:

$$
\Phi = EA=\frac{q_{enc}}{\epsilon_0}
$$

- Note that the electric field inside a conductor is 0.

Flux of a uniform field through a slanted surface.
$$
EA\cos(\theta)
$$
- Let $\theta$ be the angle between the vector perpendicular to the surface and the vector of the field.













For point charges and surfaces that a


$$
\Phi = \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A}
$$
$$
\oint f(x,y,z) = \iint_S f(\vec{r}(u,v))||\vec{r_u} \times \vec{r_v}||dA
$$
- Let $\vec{r}(u,v)$ be the equation for the shape $SS
- Let $||\vec{r_u} \times \vec{r_v}||$ be the area of the shape $S$
### Electric Flux of a Point Charge in a Sphere

Let $\rho$ be the radius of the sphere
Let $u = \phi$
Let $v = \theta$
$r=\langle \theta, \phi, \rho \rangle$
$r_u=\langle 0, 1,0 \rangle$
$r_v=\langle 1, 0, 0 \rangle$

$||r_u \times r_v|| = 1$

$\vec{E}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}$

$$
\Phi = \iint \vec{E}(r) ||\vec{r_u} \times \vec{r_v}||
$$
$$
\Phi = \int\limits_0^\pi \int\limits_0^{2\pi} \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{\rho^2} d\theta d\phi
$$
