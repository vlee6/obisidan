**Paper essentially discusses recent innovations in intergroup contact discovered in other works, arguing that it's more nuanced than Gordon Allport's initial findings.**

- Extended contact, knowing that someone ingroup has had contact with someone outgroup
- Vicarious contact, exposure to outgroup through media (TV?)
- Imagined contact, as the name suggests
- Virtual contact, contact online

Intergroup iteractions and categorization plays a large role in intergroup relations.

Intergroup contact is less effective in improving the perception of the outgroup if the interaction is with someone seen as atypical of the outgroup.

Majority group seeks to be "affirmed as moral", minority group seeks "empowerment".

May be explicit outcomes (controllable) and implicit outcomes (subconscious) to intergroup contact. Quantity (more so than qualtiy) is important to form implicit outcomes.

Understanding intergroup contact is important in the medical space.

Iintergroup contact does not necessarily improve welfare or lead to structural change. Ironically, positive intergroup contact may make minority group members less motivated to "participate in collective action to improve their position in society". However, this isn't always true. Also, negative contact might motivate people to challeneg the status quo.
