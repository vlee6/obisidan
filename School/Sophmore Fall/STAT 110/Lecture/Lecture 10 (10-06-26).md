### Poisson Approximation
Given independent events $A_1, A_2, ..., A_n$
$X$ counts the number of events.

$$X = \sum\limits^n_{j=1} I(A_j)$$
$$\text{The sum of the indicator variables for all the events}$$
Assume $P(A_j)=p_j$ is small for all $j$. The smaller the probability, the better the approximation

Then:
$$X \dot{\sim} \text Pois(\lambda) \text{, where } \lambda = E(X)=p_1+p_2+...+p_n$$

### Adding Poissons
Given $X \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda)$ and $Y \sim \text{Pois}(\mu)$
$X+Y \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda + \mu)$

### Chicken and Egg story
A hen lays random variable $N$ eggs. (We don't know how many eggs the hen laid)
$N \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda)$

$X = \text{\# of eggs that hatch}$
$Y = \text{\# of eggs that don't hatch}$
Where hatching probability $p$ is independent

If we knew there were $n$ eggs:
$X | (N=n) \sim \text{Bin}(n,p)$

$X$ and $Y$ are indpendent.
$X \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda p)$
$Y \sim \text{Pois}(\lambda q)$
