**In one or two paragraphs please share in what ways Artificial Intelligence (AI) impacts your life today, and how you see AI's potential to create positive change.**

AI has made it easier than ever to turn ideas into working products, but the ability to build something is no longer a reliable indicator for understanding. As a student, I have to balance the convenience of AI with my desire to learn, using it to understand new concepts and automate tasks I’ve already mastered while remaining deliberate about the work I still need to do myself. For example, even though my computing hardware class permits AI use in our lab assignments, I prefer to hand-roll my testbenches to build my SystemVerilog chops and ensure I understand the intricacies of timing and combinational logic. However, I regularly use LLMs to generate targeted syntax examples, verify my understanding, and bounce ideas around. In the same way the internet reduced the barrier to accessing information, AI has made personalized learning more available than ever, offering explanations tailored to my questions in ways that datasheets or traditional documentation often cannot. I like to think of AI as a personal teaching assistant, but just as a good TA won't simply give you the answers to a homework problem, you have to resist the temptation to copy and paste AI generated solutions.

My approach also differs from that of a professional engineer. While my goal as a student is to absorb as much information as possible, professionals need to prioritize deadlines and deliverables. Likewise, I expect my approach to evolve alongside my skills and priorities: as I gain mastery of a subject, I become more comfortable abstracting those details away. When building a website to document my projects, for instance, I'm happy to let AI generate CSS so I can focus on telling the story behind what I've built. Ultimately, AI has changed not only how quickly I can build things, but also how deliberately I must approach learning. It can be an incredible tool for productivity and education, provided we know when to lean on it and when to think for ourselves.
